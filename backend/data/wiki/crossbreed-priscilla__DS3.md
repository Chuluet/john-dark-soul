# Crossbreed Priscilla (DS3)

At some point Priscilla became the "Lady" of the Painted World and its inhabitants, continuing to welcome the forlorn who were led into the cold world by the heretical tales of corvian storytellers. She gifted them great scythes in the image of her own Lifehunt Scythe which would come to symbolize a distant nostalgia for the Painting natives. The humanoid corvians who found refuge in the Painting formed a small village, complete with knights and a library, where they developed a proficient understanding of the First Flame and the Dark Soul. Whenever their world showed signs of corruption, they used the Flame to "restore" the Painting, to avoid the fate of the stagnating outside world. For this purpose a "Father" was chosen among the corvians, who presided over the Flame in the Painted World to burn it when the time came, and whose successor would prepare the next painting by restoring it.

Priscilla likely became the progenitor of a line of World Painters, the extant one possibly her own daughter, to whom she imparted advanced knowledge on the First Flame and the Dark Soul. The current Painter seemingly has the ability to harness the Flame and the pigment of the Dark Soul to create Painted Worlds.

## Theories

It is possible to theorize that Gwyn had a relationship with Priscilla and from their union Gwyndolin was born, the last son of the king of the gods.
Gwyndolin is the last child of Gwyn but Yorshka, a half-dragon, is his younger sister, confirming that they share a mother. Since both siblings possess draconic traits, their common mother should have possessed draconic traits herself for them to inherit them partially.
Gwyndolin's proficiency in moon sorceries can be explained by him having inherited the unique magic of Seath's moonlight, a peculiar trait given the lineage of Gwyn otherwise connected to the power of the Sun.
Gwyndolin's final thoughts of a "white girl", as he was devoured by Aldrich, allow the creation of a miracle that replicates the Lifehunt Scythe of Priscilla; as miracles are tales of the gods, it would mean that Gwyndolin had a special relationship with Priscilla and remembered her before his demise.
Gwyndolin's legitimacy as a deity came only from the fact that he was the son of King Gwyn, implying that his matrilineage alone would not have had him considered among the deities, as she was not one.
Gwyndolin being Priscilla's son, as she was hated and feared by the gods, would explain why Gwyn would dress, educate and raise him to be a female, as the king of the gods would not have wanted him to be able to ascend the throne with his dangerous lineage.

Priscilla is likely the daughter of Seath and Velka for several reasons:
Priscilla's body is biologically infused with occult power, and Velka is capable of manipulating this power to infuse the weapons of her priests.
As Velka was considered a rogue/heretical deity by the other Gods, it would stand to reason that her offspring would exhibit certain traits of the occult. Velka was also known to enable her priests' experimentation with sorceries that drew on intelligence.
The Painted World of Ariamis is closely tied to Velka, as many artifacts and enemies found here are related to her, and therefore, it's conceivable that Velka herself commissioned the painting in order to hide Priscilla.
The structure inhabited by Priscilla resembles an old church, fitting for a half-god. It may also have been intended as a place of worship for Velka.
Priscilla would later become the Lady of said world, perhaps inheriting the title from the would-be original owner, Velka.
Even if Velka was not herself involved in the creation of the Painting, the tendency of Velka's artifacts and cultists to end up in the Painted World, which welcomed those without a place in the realm of Gods, hints towards some correlation between Velka and Priscilla.
