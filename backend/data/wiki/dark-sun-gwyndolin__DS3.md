# Dark Sun Gwyndolin (DS3)

Gwyndolin eventually left Gwyn's mausoleum, leaving behind the ring that made him take on feminine features, symbolizing a decisive change of mentality and the will to take action. Having re-forged his identity as Gwyn's son and his legitimate heir, Gwyndolin thus declared himself "Chief Deity" as his right, taking the title away from the impostor that was Lloyd. Now at the command of the gods, Gwyndolin decided to restore prosperity to what remained of his kingdom, tracing the descendants of the gods thanks to their peculiar features and returning them to their ancestral home. Irithyll of the Boreal Valley was in fact built on the ruins of Anor Londo: a new society made up of noble descendants of the gods that worshipped the moon.

Gwyndolin became the main figure of authority and worship in the new Anor Londo and a large cathedral was built to venerate the new king of the gods in the center of the city, with statues depicting him with his serpentine features and face covered and with the rose window depicting the symbol of his crown. His important role notwithstanding, Gwyndolin remained humble, preferring to live in a manor among the other similar homes in Irithyll. The building was protected by the remaining Silver Knights who had served him for ages, and the god accumulated in his home several artifacts from the history of Lordran and Drangleic and preserved paintings that told their story. Gwyndolin also created a cold climate around the region, ensuring that the moon always shone above Irithyll, using magic so powerful that it even infused the blue moon bugs native to the Boreal Valley and led to the evolution of local vegetation.

During all this time Gwyndolin continued to manage his covenant of the Blades of the Darkmoon which continued to exist from the age of the ancient royal gods of Anor Londo. The followers of this order were raised as elite knights who acted shrouded in shadows, and continued to protect the interests of the gods and hunt down their foes. These knights were therefore no longer mere Undead who followed the divinity of the moon, but a true order with a specific structure, formation and training, even adopting a standard armor treated with silver with a feminine silhouette similar to that of Gwyndolin and capable of offering protection from magic and darkness; their talismans scaled with intelligence and were compatible with Dark, as they were educated in these arts as a precaution for the fearsome enemies they would have to face. Gwyndolin's followers continueed the tradition of cutting off an ear from the corps of the sinners they vanquished, bringing them to their god as proof of their task accomplished, receiving Gwyndolin's ring and learning of his miracle.

At some point Gwyndolin and his Darkmoon Knights came into contact with the faith of the Way of Blue that had originated in Drangleic and was now rooted in Lothric in particular. Having recognized their common origin, an accord was reached: the Way of Blue recognized Gwyndolin as their ancient divinity, adopting the symbolism of the moon for their covenant; and in exchange the Darkmoon Knights protected the members threatened by dark spirits. However, the Way of Blue was already tied to an agreement with the Blue Sentinels, the original Darkmoon Knights, from the time of Drangleic in which they were in charge of their protection. Much later, Gwyndolin and his Darkmoon Knights formed a second, separate accord with the Blue Sentinels. They remained an independent organization but strongly linked to that of the Blades of the Darkmoon to the point of being almost identical. The Blue Sentinels continued to perform the same task as the Blades of the Darkmoon and protect the members of the Way of Blue but they also gained the same symbol as the Darkmoon Knights, i.e. the sword and the moon, and the same tradition of cutting off an ear from the corpses of the sinners they vanquished and even received the same rewards as the knights serving Gwyndolin, implying how their organization was kept separate only to allow the god to have a wider margin of action and more soldiers.

One day even his sister Gwynevere returned to her ancient home. The goddess, adorned with her crown, was venerated with multiple statues throughout Irithyll, where she mothered several heavenly children such as Rosaria, Mother of Rebirth and the Dancer of the Boreal Valley. The goddess returned to her location in the old cathedral of Anor Londo, guarded by the remaining Silver Knights who had once protected her illusion. At some point, Gwyndolin welcomed into Irithyll a young crossbreed girl with neither a name nor knowledge of the world, recognizing her as his sister from the same mother. The two siblings developed an affectionate bond, with the god giving her a sacred chime and even the name of "Yorshka". Eventually a church was built in her name within Irithyll, exactly where the Painted World of Ariamis was once kept, in which she was venerated as a goddess by the humans who worked in the city.

## Sulyvahn's Coup and the Downfall

One day Sulyvahn, a young sorcerer, arrived in Irithyll from the Painted World and was welcomed among the inhabitants despite not being a descendant of the gods, earning Gwyndolin's favor thanks to a crude replica of his magical catalyst presented to his Darkmoon Knights, demonstrating the young man's talent. Eventually Sulyvahn discovered the Profaned Capital deep within the region and the Profaned Flame contained therein, an unfading flame that became the fuel and instrument of his ambitions. Armed with this new power, the young sorcerer thus climbed the ranks of Irithyll's clergy, corrupting the city's holy knights with the everlasting flame and transforming their soldiers into his spies and assassins, effectively enforcing his way through their highest ranks.

At some point Gwyndolin was "stricken by illness" and forced to retreat to the old cathedral of Anor Londo, entrusting his sister Yorshka with command of the covenant of the Blades of the Darkmoon and the leading of its knights against the enemies of the gods and to watch over those who linked the First Flame. Yorshka continued accepting the proofs of their accomplished duty and rewarding them with the miracles of Gwyndolin, even without knowledge of their meaning. However, Sulyvahn exploited the frail situation, declaring himself Pontiff and becoming the tyrant of Irithyll, leveraging the influence he already had among the clergy and knights of the city. The man thus imprisoned Yorshka on the tower of her own church, isolating her from the world and holding her hostage, unable to hear any news of her brother. Afterwards Sulyvahn took Gwyndolin himself hostage, locking him up in the old cathedral, cementing his position as Pontiff, the "voice of god", to reign through his figure, while also preventing unwanted foreign interventions.

As the First Flame began to fade Prince Lothric, who had been raised from birth to become a Lord of Cinder, refused to do so, leading the kingdom of Lothric to resurrect four dead Lords of Cinder from their graves to once again be used as fuel. Among them was Aldrich, once a cleric of Irithyll turned cannibal and saint of his own faith, who had transformed into an abysmal monstrosity due to his immeasurable hunger for humans that had made him "worthy" of linking the Flame. Like most of the resurrected Lords of Cinder, Aldrich had no intention of sacrificing himself anew, as he had come to believe that an "Age of the Deep" should come and replace the Age of Fire, motivating him to try to devour the gods themselves to erode their hold on the Flame and accelerate the coming of his vision. Gathering an army of deacons from the Cathedral of the Deep, Aldrich, still hungry for flesh and now eager to devour Gods, marched on Irithyll with his followers so that he could devour Gwyndolin, the king of the Gods. He found his way blocked by Sulyvahn and his knights, as the Pontiff had no intention of relinquishing the source of his control over the city and the Way of White without a fight. Aldrich emerged victorious from the ensuing battle, slaughtering Sulyvahn's troops and forcing the Pontiff to reluctantly clear the way for the ruined cathedral of Anor Londo. Having finally reached his prey, Aldrich began to slowly devour Gwyndolin, who was most likely powerless to fight back, and consumed his soul, gaining access to his memories of Priscilla through his dreams.

An Unkindled, in their quest to bring another Lord of Cinder to their throne, found Aldrich within the now-decrepit Anor Londo Cathedral, still consuming the deity. The body of the dying god was puppeteered by Aldrich into using Gwyndolin's powerful sorceries in a final battle. Once the Unkindled finally slew Aldrich, Dark Sun Gwyndolin was released from his gruesome fate, ending his torment and allowing him to pass in some semblance of peace.

## Theories

It is possible to theorize that Gwyndolin is the result of the union of Gwyn and Crossbreed Priscilla for several reasons:
Gwyndolin is the last child of Gwyn; but Yorshka, a half-dragon, is his younger sister, confirming that they share a mother. Since the two siblings both possess draconic traits, their common mother should have possessed draconic traits herself for them to inherit them partially.
Gwyndolin, despite being another children of Gwyn, did not inherit the power of Sunlight like the rest of his father's lineage. Instead he inherited the magic of the moon that made him a powerful moon sorcerer, a power that was unique to the white dragon Seath, the probable father of Priscilla.
Gwyndolin's final thoughts of a "white girl", as he was devoured by Aldrich, allow the creation of a miracle that replicates the Lifehunt Scythe of Priscilla, and, as miracles are tales of the gods, it would mean that Gwyndolin had a special relationship with Priscilla and remembered her before his death
Alternatively, Gwyndolin's final thoughts could be directed to Yorshka, another "pale girl", hidden by Sulyvahn. If Yorshka inherited her mother's power, she could have possessed the Lifehunt Scythe miracle as well.
Gwyndolin being Priscilla's son, as she was hated and feared by the gods, would explain why Gwyn would dress, educate and raise him to be a female, as the king of the gods would never have wanted him to be able to ascend the throne with his dangerous lineage.
Gwyndolin's legitimacy as a deity came only from the fact that he was the son of King Gwyn, implying that his matrilineage alone would not have made him part of the deities, as she was not one.

It is possible to theorize that the ritual combat of the "Battle of Stoicism" was created in Oolacile by Gwyndolin as a prototype of the more complicated Firelinking system that would later include the Bells of Awakening and Sen's Fortress. In fact, the victorious warriors were welcomed to Anor Londo, presumably to be tested again before obtaining the Lordvessel. It should be noted that Gwyn had entrusted her sons to continue the path of Firelink and guide the humans to maintain the Age of Fire.

It is possible to theorize that, although Gwyn's Firstborn had been disinherited, stripped of his deific stature and eradicated from history, Gwyndolin, as the new king of the gods, tried to get closer to his brother by recalling him to the new city created for the descendants of the gods. In fact, an altar without a statue in the center of the city of Irithyll contains the Ring of the Sun's First Born that he himself had left in front of his father's Mausoleum when he left Anor Londo, implying that Gwyndolin had prepared a place to welcome his older brother back into the pantheon of gods and allow again his worship if he ever returned.

Possible relations between Gwyndolin and the Ringed City are unclear; although he is never directly mentioned within, and its inhabitants claim to serve Gwyn, there is a statue of Gwyndolin in a cathedral of the Ringed City. However, as the city was partially built to further the worship of its inhabitants towards Gwyn's clan, the statue's construction might have taken place long before the events of Dark Souls. Whether Gwyndolin knew or not of the Ringed City's existence, and if he had access to it, is therefore unclear.
