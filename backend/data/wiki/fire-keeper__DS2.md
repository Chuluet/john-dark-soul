# Fire Keeper (DS2)

Fire Keepers are a storyline concept and group of characters in the Dark Souls series.

They are female characters who watch over bonfires and provide assistance to the Undead and Unkindled in their quests.

## General information

In Dark Souls, bonfires watched over by Fire Keepers are automatically kindled to provide ten Estus Flask charges each. Still they may be upgraded through the Rite of Kindling, just like other bonfires. All Fire Keepers have special souls and drop a Fire Keeper Soul upon death. Fire Keepers universally share the ability to upgrade Estus Flasks using the souls of their fellow Fire Keepers. The death or departure of a Fire Keeper will lead to the bonfire they attended to be extinguished, and it can no longer be used to rest, level up or teleport from using the Lordvessel. The description of the regular Fire Keeper Soul states that "Each Fire Keeper is a corporeal manifestation of her bonfire, and a draw for the humanity which is offered to her."

In Dark Souls II, the three Fire Keepers in Things Betwixt and the Emerald Herald, believed by some to be Fire Keeper, can be killed without the nearby bonfire being affected. The way the Herald can level up the Bearer of the Curse is reminescent of how the Fire Keepers in Dark Souls III can the Ashen One. One of the Keepers, Strowen, offers the player to re-allocate their attributes in exchange for a Soul Vessel.

In Dark Souls III, the unnamed Fire Keeper can level up the Ashen One like the Hemerald did in the previous game. She is also immortal; killing her, in addition to not causing the Shrine bonfire to be affected, will only cause her to respawn upon reloading the area, claiming that she cannot die. Instead of a Keeper, it is Rosaria, Mother of Rebirth, the leader of the Rosaria's Fingers covenant, who allows the player to re-allocate their attributes.

All confirmed Fire Keepers in the series, as well as prospective Fire Keeper Irina of Carim and the possible Fire Keepers that are the Emerald Herald and mentionned fourth sister in Dark Souls II, are female, as are seemingly all of the dead Fire Keepers whose bodies can be found in the Firelink Shrine of Dark Souls III. The description of the generic Fire Keeper Soul in Dark Souls similarly refers to "Each Fire Keeper" using female pronouns. Whether the reason is cultural or biological is unclear.

All Fire Keepers in the series suffer substantial ailments: Anastacia has neither tongue nor feet, Quelaag's Sister is gravely ill in addition to being twisted by the Flame of Chaos, and the Darkmoon Knightess has a "ghastly form" and "hideous swarms of humanity" hidden under her armor, while the unnamed Dark Souls III Fire Keeper, seemingly all of her Lothric predecessors, the Old Ladies, and Irina are all blind. The Emerald Herald, who might be a Keeper, has no visible affliction, although she claims that she "did not come out as intended".

Lore
In Lordran
As the First Flame naturally became to fade away, announcing an Age of Dark, Gwyn, Lord of Sunlight, the ruler of the Gods of Anor Londo, became the first to "link" the First Flame, sacrificing himself to become a Lord of Cinder and prolong the First Flame by rekindling it anew. He handed down the power of the sun to his children and his throne to his firstborn son, and either following Gwyn's instructions or of their own volition, the Gods of Anor Londo would devise a "prophecy" so that one day, another would amass power similar to that of Gwyn, reach the First Flame and link it to further prolong the Age of Fire.

Fire Keepers played a crucial role in the fullfilling of this prophecy, as they would keep to bonfires. Perhaps most crucial among bonfires was the one of Firelink Shrine, an entry point into Lordran for Undead seeking to fulfill the prophecy and a pivotal crossroads leading into various areas key to their quest. This bonfire which was attended by Anastacia of Astora. Anastacia was mute, her tongue likely removed, and she believed herself "impure" for unknown reasons. It is unclear if she became Fire Keeper of her own volition or was forced to. She saw the fulfilment of the prophecy as salvation, and the end of the undead curse as something that would allow her to die a human.

Another Fire Keeper, whose name is unknown, was the "Fair Lady" who was one of the Witch of Izalith's "Daughters of Chaos" and the sister of Chaos Witch Quelaag. She lived in Quelaag's Domain, in a chamber hidden behind an illusory wall, in front of a bonfire she attended. Queelag and her sister had both been transformed into partial demons by the Flame of Chaos accidentally created by their mother, and the Fair Lady, who was kind and benevolent, had an entourage of servants whose numbers counted many who suffered from a parasitic disease, which the Fair Lady tried to help with against Queelag's will, severely weakening herself in the process. Her words cannot be understood by traditional means.

The third and last confirmed Fire Keeper in Lordran is the "Darkmoon Knightess", who attends the first bonfire found in Anor Londo by Undead seeking to fulfill the prophecy. Donning a Brass Set gifted by Dark Sun Gwyndolin, Gwyn's son and the last God left in Lordran ,of whom she is a faithful follower, she is a member of his Blade of the Darkmoon covenant that seeks and punishes sinners. Unlike Anastacia and the Fair Lady, she is capable of speaking, but is rather untalkative, only delivering some guidance to help fulfil the prophecy and inviting Undead to proceed deeper into Anor Londo to meet the Gwyndolin-created illusion of his sister Gwynevere. If the Undead is successful in this task, she will provide them information on Seath the Scaleless, the nearest of the four powerful entities that the Undead must slay to fulfill the prophecy. She is notably the only Fire Keeper in the series to belong to a covenant, the only one to be a fighter, and the only one who can be made hostile even if she is not attacked, as offending the Gods of Anor Londo will make her attack the player.

Deep below Lordran is Ash Lake. In the area furthest away from the point from which it is accessed is a bonfire looked over by the Stone Dragon, descendant of the Everlasting Dragons. The bonfire looked over by the Stone Dragon is already lit and kindled, providing 10 Estus Flask charges. It is possible that the Dragon, who does not speak, is a Fire Keeper; as it cannot be killed, moved or made hostile, the possible connection between the Dragon and the bonfire cannot be directly established.

In Drangleic
Although the status of Fire Keepers at the times of the events of Dark Souls II is unknown, they seem mostly absent from Drangleic. Three Fire Keepers named Strowen, Morrel, and Griant inhabit Things Betwixt, an area connecting Drangleic to the rest of the world, where they and their housekeeper Milibeth welcome newcomers to Drangleic, most of whom come in the hopes of finding the cure to Undeath that the kingdom is rumored to hold. There was a fourth sister, presumably also a Fire Keeper, although her identity is unclear.

In the town of Majula, a woman known as the Emerald Herald awaits the arrival of Undead into Drangleic, to guide and assist them on a quest to seek Vendrick, the King of Drangleic, and gain the power of his royal throne, the Throne of Want, which would allow the newly-crowned Undead King to chose the fate of the First Flame and, by extension, the world.

The origin and nature of the Herald is unclear, although when the Bearer of the Curse's quest is near its complexion, she will reveal some of story, claiming that her name is Shanalotte, "born of dragons, contrived by men" for the purpose of assisting the linking of the First Flame. However, she "did not come out as intended", and as a result the First Flame was not linked before it started to fade enough that the curse of Undeath started to plague Drangleic as it once did Lordran.

The Herald is not outright called a Fire Keeper, however she plays the same role as the Fire Keepers of Lothric later would, helping draw out the strength of Undeads in the closest they have to a home base as they gather the power needed to reach the First Flame, and claims that she was created to help the linking of the First Flame. Royal Sorcerer Navlaan recognizes the Herald as someone of importance, and acknowledges that some believe her to be "the last Fire Keeper", although he is doubtful of any rumor. When the Bearer is ready to seize the Throne of Want, the Herald might declare her journey complete, as she is certain that the Bearer will succeed and no longer needs her guidance.It is of note that although she seemingly favors the Linking of the First Flame, she does not attempt to convince the Bearer to do so, recognizing that they alone must decide whether to renew the Flame or let it die.

In Lothric's time
In Lothric
At a certain point before the events of Dark Souls III'', the First Flame began to fade as naturally occurs at the end of every renewed Age of Fire, and the first Fire Keeper in Lothric's history had a vision of the end of the Age of Fire and the beginning of an Age of Dark. Ludleth, an exile from the kingdom of Courland and a future Lord of Cinder, worked with Lothric to ensure that all Fire Keepers to come would be deprived of their eyes to prevent them from observing a future without the First Flame. The original eyes were hidden in a version of Lothric's Firelink Shrine in a distant time.

As the First Flame lost most of its original power, linking the First Flame became necessary more and more often, and the Firelink Shrine of Lothric was used a number of times to help would-be Lords of Cinder, always with the assistance of a Fire Keeper who would be permanently blinded on purpose. These Fire Keepers were incapable of dying for as long as the First Flame remained fading, and striking them down would only cause them to be dead for a short time. Only once a new Lord of Cinder had linked the First Flame would the Fire Keeper who had assisted them be killed, her body laid to rest in a tower by the side of Lothric's Firelink Shrine.

Among those Fire Keepers of Lothric was one "who is said to have returned from the Abyss". Uncharacteristically, this Fire Keeper accepted the Dark Sigil that represents the dark nature of humanity embraced by Londor that is contrary to what the Fire Keepers of Lothric stand for. What happened to her ultimately is unknown, although her soul ended up atop the tower in which the bodies of the Fire Keepers of old are kept.

Prince Lothric, the second son of Oceiros, the King of Lothric, was groomed from birth to become the next Lord of Cinder. However, influenced by the "First of the Scholars", he refused to become a Lord of Cinder. As a result, the other powers aiming for further prolongation of the Age of Fire exhumed four Lords of Cinder from their graves: Ludleth, the Abyss Watchers of Farron, Aldrich, Saint of the Deep, and Yhorm the Giant. These Lords were meant to sacrifice themselves to link the Flame once again, using the embers of the Flame which remained within themselves as kindling, but all except for Ludleth refused the call and instead settled back into their old domains. As a last resort, the Unkindled, failed Lords of Cinder who were consumed by the First Flame, were resurrected to seek the absconded Lords of Cinder and link the First Flame, assisted by the current Fire Keeper of Lothric's Firelink Shrine, including the Ashen One, Anri of Astora, Hawkwood the Deserter, Horace the Hushed and Siegward of Catarina..

Once all five Lords "sat" their thrones, meaning that Prince Lothric and the three Lords of Cinder who fled were slain, and their cinders, together with the willing Ludleth's, were offered to Firelink Shrine, an Unkindled Champion would reach the Kiln of the First Flame to overcome the final challenge of the Soul of Cinder and decide the fate of the First Flame.

## In Carim

The Way of White religion is very prominent in the kingdom of Carim, leading to the training of maidens as magicians. The frail Irina of Carim was one such maiden, and became a prospective Fire Keeper; she was also blind, although whether or not she lost her eyes as part of her path to becoming a Fire Keeper is unknown; she is the only evidence in the lore that at least some Fire Keepers are not directly born such.

Drawing inspiration from legendary Carim knight Morne's dedication to the goddess Caitha, every knight of Carim dedicated their entire career to a single maiden, and the knight Eygon was assigned to Irina's protection. The two journey'd to Lothric, where Irina was to become a fully-fledged Fire Keeper. However, a "grand treachery" from Irina led the two to consider the quest failed by her fault, and Eygon was seemingly left with little esteem for his assigned maiden, whom he considers "a lost cause" and "beyond repair", although he promised her that he would end her suffering should she lose her mind to the "darkness" that threatened to overcome her. Although he did not technically betray his oath to protect her, Eygon ended up patiently waiting outside a cell in the Undead Settlement containing a confused and highly distressed Irina, unbothered with rescuing her, and perhaps having locked her up himself; Irina thinks herself alone, seemingly believing Eygon to have completely abandonned her.

The Ashen One can come upon Eygon and the imprisoned Irina in the Undead Settlement, where they can free Irina and accept her services, after which Eygon will declare themselves the Ashen One's ally for as long as Irina is safe.

Irina's questline can end in two ways: her "good" questline takes place if the Ashen One provides her with all non-dark miracle tomes and purchases the associated miracles. This will lead Irina to finally achieve her dream of becoming a Fire Keeper, relocating to the bottom of one of the towers outside the Shrine, where the Ashen One can trade souls to further their strength as with the shrine's assigned Fire Keeper; unlike the other Fire Keeper, Irina does not respawn if she is killed, possibly because she has not been tasked by Lothric to act as Fire Keeper for the current intended Linking of the First Flame.

The second outcome of Irina's questline happens if any dark miracle is purchased from her after giving her a forbidden tome, leading her to succumb to "darkness" and Eygon to kidnap her in a rescue attempt from the Ashen One. After Eygon is slain outside Firelink Shrine, Irina will return inside, although she will then be too lost to the "darkness" to provide her services as a miracle merchant. She will not even be able to feel the Ashen One's touch, unless they are wearing Eygon's gauntlets, which lead her to mistake them for Eygon and asking "Eygon" to fulfill his promise to mercy kill her.

## In Londor

Londor was the "Land of Hollows", where Undead humans had accepted and embraced Hollowing. Renouncing the First Flame and following the wishes of the primordial serpent Darkstalker Kaathe, the Sable Church of Londor, led by sisters Yuria and Liliane (as well as at originally a third sister, Elfriede), aimed to bring about the long-delayed Age of Dark, making them drastically opposed to the goals of Fire Keeper throughout the ages, whose purposes were to help fight hollowing and support the Age of Fire.

The "pilgrims of Londor" are exiles sent specifically to Lothric so that they would "die", although their nature and purpose of both the pilgrims and the deaths they seek is unclear. Among them was Yoel of Londor, who "failed" to die upon reaching Lothric, and can be found still alive among the dead bodies of other pilgrims in the Undead Settlement by the Ashen One. Little is known about Yoel besides the fact that he is a pilgrim and was once a sorcerer. However, he also has a rare talent, one that in theory is exclusive to Fire Keeper: the ability to "Draw Out True Strength", which is to say he can make the Ashen One stronger. Furthermore, unlike the Fire Keeper or the Emerald Herald, he does not require a cost in souls to do so, and instead brands the Ashen One, the service of whom he can enter, with a Dark Sigil such as the one wore by the Fire Keeper who had entered the Abyss every time, as a way to help prepare the Ashen One's ascension as the "Lord of Hollows" to bring about the Age of Dark. Given that the Emerald Herald was rumored a Fire Keeper and that Yoel is male, this marks him as the only character very likely not to be a Fire Keeper yet still able to draw out the strength of an Undead. He claims to be able to do so because "We pilgrims of Londor are keenly aware. That those branded by the Darksign possess something quite special", indicating that perhaps others in Londor have the same ability.
