# Gwyn, Lord of Cinder (DS2)

thumb|A distorted representation of Gwyn in Heide. 
Ages past his linking of the First Flame, Gwyn's very name was lost. Even his miracles recounting his battles with lightning spears against the dragons at the start of the Age of Fire do not mention his name, implying that his heritage and culture have been lost to Drangleic. In fact, the only knowledge of a "god of the sun" leading the clan of the gods comes from the miracles that narrate the deeds of his son, who became king and god of the sun after him but was then exiled and deprived of his name.

However in Heide, as the beacon of the culture of Anor Londo in the region, there are several statues representing him, albeit in a distorted form but resembling the characteristics of Gwyn's representations in Lordran. Although portrayed with the head of an eagle, the pose, the sun medallion and the sword still strongly resemble the weapon, robes and symbology of the Lord of Sunlight.

Centuries after Gwyn sacrificed his soul to link the Flame, some remnants of it manifested again in Drangleic, continuing to exert their influence on the region. At some point the Old Iron King came into contact with the soul of Gwyn, which had become "Ichorous Earth" and produced the scorching iron and magma of the Iron Keep. The Old Iron King was thus destroyed by the mass of iron possessed by the soul of Gwyn who then went on to control the remains of the Old Iron King, creating a demonic-looking creature made of melted iron.

thumb|The remnants of Gwyn's soul manifesting one last time. 
Aldia, Scholar of the First Sin at some point learned of Gwyn's existence and his story but not his name, referring to him merely as the "Lord of Light". He understood how Gwyn altered the natural order and began linking the Flame, which extended the Age of Fire against the course of nature, and he expresses his outrage at Gwyn for making men lesser than their original form as the "Lord of Light" banished Dark and all that stemmed from Humanity with the Darksign, defining it as the "First Sin" committed against humans and their nature.
