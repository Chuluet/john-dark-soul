# Livid Pyromancer Dunnel (DS3)

is a mad dark spirit in the Dark Souls III: Ashes of Ariandel DLC.

## Location

He is found in the Painted World of Ariandel, on top of the tower-like structure situated above the Champion's Gravetender and Gravetender Greatwolf boss arena. This area can only be accessed by a hidden path in the basement of the Ariandel Chapel.

## Plot

Dunnel is an optional encounter that can be battled if the player is a Host of Embers while traversing his invasion area. He will always appear, even after defeating the two bosses in the Painted World of Ariandel, something unusual for Dark Souls III invaders. If the player manages to defeat him they'll obtain his unique pyromancy Floating Chaos, and they'll also be able to loot the Pyromancer's Parting Flame more easily.

## Lore

Dunnel was a pyromancer from the Great Swamp. He travelled far from his home in order to reach the Smouldering Lake, where he witnessed the cerimonies of the Demon Clerics who still stalked the decrepit halls, growing fascinated with their arts and recreating them in the form of a unique pyromancy.

Eventually, the pyromancer would find companionship with someone who was considered unseemely or disgraceful. Whether this encounter happened before or after his arrival in the Painted World of Ariandel remains a mystery. It's clear, however, that Dunnel's partner would perish within the Painted World, as he would offer his own pyromancy flame as a farewell gift for their departure. Shortly after doing so, his grief turned him into a mad spirit, cursed to wander the painting's landscape.

His phantom seems to manifest inside a derelict tower-like structure that resembles an old building from the Painted World of Ariamis. This is where the Ashen One would finally put him to rest, hopefully reuniting him with his beloved.

## Artifacts

Floating Chaos
 Pyromancer's Parting Flame

## Theories

It's possible that Dunnel's "hideous spouse" is actually Crossbreed Priscilla, the mistress of the Painted World. Despite her cult following within the painting, the outside world considered her an "abomination" due to her occult abilities. Since the Japanese terminology could be talking about more than just the spouse's physical appearance, there's a possibility the "hideous" part of it refers to the way she was viewed by the outside world as shameful, just as the Crossbreed once was.

The place where Dunnel performed his farewell cerimony is also reminicent of Priscilla's boss arena in the original Dark Souls, meaning his partner had to have perished near the structure before he transformed into a mad spirit. Dunnel's own connection to death through his Japanese title of "Livor Mortis" Pyromancer and the parting flame rite appear to be rather interesting too, as Priscilla's power of lifehunt made those of the outside world believe she was an antithesis to life itself.

This is all just speculation however, to be taken with a grain of salt.
