# Yhorm the Giant (DS3)

Yhorm the Giant is a boss in Dark Souls III and one of the five Lords of Cinder encountered by the Ashen One in Dark Souls III.

## Location

Found at the end of the Profaned Capital, in a large throne room located between two treasury rooms guarded by Jailer Handmaids, Mimics, and Gargoyles.

## Description

Yhorm is a hulking giant, comparable in size to the Iron Golem from Dark Souls or the Giant Lord from Dark Souls II.

He is armed with an enormous machete, his eyes glow red and his face appears skeletal. Similar to other Lords of Cinder, his body and armor is covered in several burning scars and cuts.

## Lore

Long ago, Yhorm, as the descendant of an ancient conqueror, was asked to lead the people once subjugated, serving as both their blade and shield. When there were those who doubted him, he gave them a giant-slaying sword, to show his trustworthiness. He risked everything for his people in the frontlines, fighting valiantly as a one-man vanguard. It is stated that he lost someone dear to him and, as a result, he forsook his greatshield. Afterwards, he notched his machete to allow for two hand use, giving him an even more ferocious fighting style. At some point, "lonely" Yhorm befriended Siegward of Catarina, who became a dear friend of his.

Due to a curse triggered by the four female relatives of an unknown oracle, which also turned them into careless greedy creatures, the Profaned Flame was unleashed, a flame that never goes out. Yhorm became a Lord of Cinder to put the Profaned Flame to rest, even as many spoke insincerely of his role. However, after he became a Lord of Cinder, the hellish flame fell out of the sky and destroyed the city, yet consuming naught but human flesh, incinerating the human inhabitants. The flame, despite deriving from Dark and the Abyss, actively sought to harm humanity.

Before linking the First Flame, he gave his other Storm Ruler to Siegward, who promised to end Yhorm's suffering if he ever returned to life.

## Theories

It is possible to theorize that the "great conqueror" from whom Yhorm descends is High Lord Wolnir, as the only character in the lore of Dark Souls III addressed as a great conqueror. In addition, Yhorm's immense size could be explained by his descent from Wolnir, also a gigantic humanoid. Again, the Catacombs of Carthus and the Profaned Capital, despite the convergence of the lands towards Lothric, are both underground and close together, implying that Wolnir had also conquered the Profaned Capital and that when Wolnir was overwhelmed by the Abyss, only his son remained as potential heir who could reign over the conquered city.

It is possible to theorize that Yhorm refused to serve as fuel for the First Flame a second time due to the fact that, once awakened from his grave, he realized that his sacrifice had been futile. The sorcerers of the Profaned Capital had continued to experiment on the Profaned Flame he had tried to quell with his sacrifice and as a result the entire city was destroyed, making his life of service meaningless even after the end. The moment he rises from the grave, he can be seen already howling in despair. In addition, he may also have simply understood the state of the world, now corrupted beyond any possibility of traditional salvation through the linking of the Fire, and simply decided to await death in the ruins of his city.

It is possible to theorize that the event in which the Profaned Flame went out of control, destroying the Profaned Capital, was the result of a Court Sorcerers ritual gone awry. In fact, these sorcerers did not care about their creations even when they were as dangerous as the dark flame, continuing to live as if nothing had happened, driven by an unethical and unruly pursuit of knowledge. In addition, the only survivors of the destruction of the city are a noble court sorcerer and the handmaids, possibly in the noble's service, implying that someone had somehow managed to avoid being devoured by the flame. Furthermore, the detail of the Profaned Flame "falling" from the sky and devouring only human beings implies an unnatural event in which the flame was not in its previous controlled conditions but was activated toward human targets.
